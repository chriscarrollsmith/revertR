Comprehensive asset analysis worksheet

This worksheet is designed for comprehensive analysis of momentum and mean reversion effects in various asset classes, using novel indicators that quantify mean reversion and momentum in terms of the price’s z-score relationship to its exponential moving averages. Note that many model parameters can be adjusted. Run all cells in the document to complete the analysis. In addition to rendering plots and figures, this will also save plot data in the folder /data.

This library and worksheet are works in progress. I have split the data for machine learning, but I have not yet attempted to validate the training results on the test data. I would also like to try some more traditional machine learning algorithms, like maybe a k-nearest neighbors (knn) algorithm. I also want to make use of my beta variable and add some additional variables, such as percentile rank and index-level z-score.

S&P 500

Analysis of S&P 500 stocks reveals that mean reversion works best on short timeframes (20-day EMAs with 20-day returns), and momentum works best on long timeframes (1000-day EMAs with 1000-day returns). It also suggests that mean-reversion effects are stronger than momentum effects, though that may just be noise from survivorship bias.

# S&P 500 (Large Cap) -----------------------------------------------------

# Get and clean S&P 500 tickers
tickers <- tq_index("SP500") %>%
  filter(symbol != "-") %>%
  pull(symbol)
Getting holdings for SP500

                             periods = c(20, 50, 100, 200, 500, 1000),
                             return_periods = c(20),
                             ma_period_to_use = 20,

                             benchmark_index_ticker = "^GSPC",

Commodities ETFs

Commodities ETFs tend to be futures ETFs. As a result, there’s a lot of “negative carry,” or “cost to own,” in the form of contract fees and rollover costs. These are meant as vehicles for short-term speculation, not long-term investing. As a result, their returns are generally negative.

As the plots show, this is also one of the only asset classes that exhibits downward momentum rather than upward mean reversion after a strong move to the downside.
